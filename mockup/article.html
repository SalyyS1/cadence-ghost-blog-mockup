London Marathon 2026

The course, the pacing plan and the small decisions that shape race day.

Thomas Bateson | 01 January 2026 | 8 min read

A good marathon plan is a collection of small decisions made early enough to matter.

London rewards patience. The opening miles are quick because the road is wide and the crowd is loud, but the race is decided much later. I arrived with one simple instruction: keep the first half calm, protect the effort, and leave room to race the final ten kilometres.

That sounds obvious until the clock shows a pace that feels comfortable. The temptation is to bank time. The better move is to bank attention. I watched the effort, the breathing and the shape of the stride before I watched the split.

The plan

The build lasted twelve weeks. Three easy runs kept the legs fresh, one controlled session added speed, and the long run stayed conversational until the final section. The goal was not to create a perfect week. It was to arrive with enough consistency that one imperfect day could not undo the work.

The race became easier when I stopped trying to win the first ten kilometres.
Thomas Bateson, race notes

Race morning

The first checkpoint came at Greenwich. I was surrounded by runners moving at every possible pace, so I used the course markers instead of the crowd as my reference. Drink early, settle the shoulders, and let the first climb pass without changing the effort.

Race note

If the first half feels fast, check the effort before changing the plan. A calm ten seconds now is worth much more than a frantic minute later.

The final ten

The hard part arrived exactly where the training suggested it would. My legs felt heavy, but the pace had not fallen away. I shortened the focus to the next street, then the next drink station, then the next runner in front of me. Small targets kept the whole distance manageable.

What I would repeat

  • Start with effort. Let the pace arrive after the body settles.
  • Practice the final section. The last kilometres deserve their own plan.
  • Keep the race morning simple. Familiar food, familiar shoes and fewer decisions.

The result was not a perfect race. It was a race where the plan survived contact with the day. That is a better standard, and one I will carry into the next start line.
